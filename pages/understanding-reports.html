Analysis

Understanding your report

Learn how to read your Rackwyse analysis — findings, severity levels, savings estimates, and how to prioritise fixes.

Report overview

After your bill is analysed, Rackwyse produces a report containing one or more findings — specific waste patterns or inefficiencies detected in your cloud spending. Each finding includes:

  • A plain-English description of the problem
  • A severity level (Critical, High, Medium, or Low)
  • An estimated monthly savings if you fix it
  • Specific steps to resolve the issue
  • The affected service or resource
💡
Start with Critical and High findings
Sort your findings by severity. Critical and High findings typically represent the largest savings opportunities and should be addressed first.

Severity levels

Every finding is assigned one of four severity levels based on the estimated monthly savings and the urgency of the issue.

SeverityTypical savingsExamples
🔴 Critical $500+/month Idle NAT Gateways, unused data transfer costs, massively oversized instances running 24/7
🟠 High $100–$500/month Unattached EBS volumes, oversized RDS instances, idle Elastic IPs
🟡 Medium $20–$100/month Underutilised reserved instances, old snapshots, unused load balancers
⚪ Low Under $20/month Old log files, minor rightsizing opportunities, unused API gateway calls

Savings estimates

Each finding shows an estimated monthly savings — the amount you could save per month if you implement the fix. These estimates are calculated based on:

  • The actual cost data from your billing export
  • Current AWS/GCP/Azure public pricing
  • Usage patterns detected in your bill (e.g. a resource with near-zero utilisation)
  • Known cost reduction benchmarks for each waste pattern type
ℹ️
Estimates are conservative
Rackwyse uses conservative estimates to avoid overpromising. Actual savings may be higher, especially for infrastructure that has grown over time without regular review.

Common finding types

Idle and unused resources

Resources that exist but are not being used — or barely used. Classic examples: EC2 instances with less than 5% average CPU utilisation, unattached EBS volumes, Elastic IP addresses not associated with a running instance.

Oversized instances

Instances provisioned with more compute, memory, or storage than your workload actually needs. Rackwyse detects these by analysing the ratio of your instance costs to your actual utilisation patterns visible in billing data.

Data transfer waste

Cloud providers charge for data transfer between regions, availability zones, and the internet. Many teams overpay significantly here — for example, inter-AZ traffic that could be avoided by placing services in the same AZ, or egress traffic that could be reduced with a CDN.

Reservation and savings plan inefficiency

Reserved Instances and Savings Plans offer up to 72% discount over on-demand pricing — but only for the specific usage they cover. If your usage has changed since you purchased reservations, you may be paying for capacity you are not using.

Storage waste

Old EBS snapshots, S3 objects in expensive storage tiers, CloudWatch log groups with no retention policy, and unused databases all contribute to storage waste that compounds over time.

Misconfigured services

Services configured for high availability or large scale when neither is needed — for example, a multi-AZ RDS instance for a development database, or a NAT Gateway used for a handful of API calls per day.

Fix instructions

Each finding includes step-by-step instructions for resolving the issue. These are specific to your cloud provider and the service affected. For example, a finding about an idle EC2 instance will include the exact steps to:

  • Identify which instance is idle
  • Verify it is safe to stop or terminate
  • Take a final snapshot if needed
  • Stop or terminate the instance
  • Verify the cost reduction on your next bill
⚠️
Always verify before making changes
Billing data alone cannot tell you whether a resource is truly safe to remove — only your team knows what each resource is used for. Always verify with your infrastructure team before stopping, terminating, or deleting any resource.

What if there are no findings?

If Rackwyse does not find any waste patterns, you will see a green message: "No waste patterns detected — your bill looks well-optimised!"

This means either your infrastructure is genuinely well-optimised, or the billing export did not contain enough detail for Rackwyse to detect patterns. In the latter case, try exporting a more detailed report (e.g. the AWS Cost & Usage Report with resource IDs enabled).
